# Mission-team operating cycle

A small team needs a repeatable way to turn evidence into work and work into evidence. Use four linked phases.

## 1. Hypothesize

State what the team believes about the customer, problem, mechanism, and expected signal. A useful hypothesis can be disproved and does not disguise a promise as a fact.

Minimum record:

- belief;
- supplied evidence;
- largest unknown;
- bounded test;
- owner and due date.

## 2. Prioritize

Choose the work most likely to change the next decision while respecting guardrails and capacity. Compare options using the same criteria. Record what will not be done and who authorized the tradeoff.

Minimum record:

- candidate work;
- expected decision value;
- effort or capacity assumption;
- dependency;
- selected work and reason;
- owner and due date.

## 3. Execute

Deliver the smallest coherent increment that can produce valid evidence or customer value. Keep routine choices inside the team's decision boundary. Escalate only when a stated threshold is crossed.

Minimum record:

- increment;
- completion evidence;
- guardrail check;
- blocker and failure path;
- owner and due date.

## 4. Analyze

Compare the observed result with the hypothesis and guardrails. Decide whether to stop, change, continue, or scale. Preserve unexpected and negative evidence.

Minimum record:

- observed result;
- evidence quality and limitations;
- guardrail result;
- decision;
- owner and next review date.

## Measures

Use one north-star measure for the mission outcome and a small number of guardrail measures for unacceptable tradeoffs. Never invent a baseline or target. If a measure is unavailable, name the evidence gap and the owner and date for resolving it.

## Cadence test

Choose a cadence that matches learning and delivery, not a standard calendar. The cycle is too slow when decisions wait for a meeting after the necessary evidence exists. It is too fast when the team repeatedly reviews noise before a meaningful signal could appear.

The operating cycle supports the team charter. It does not replace mission clarity, dedicated capacity, or decision rights.
